A robot you can actually play air hockey against.

Context

Build 18 is a one-week build sprint at CMU, and my team went in wanting an air hockey robot that could see the puck and play on its own. A week is not much time, so we scaled the dream down to something we could actually finish: a robot opponent we controlled ourselves. We got it working, and it won the Lab Rats award for the team that put in the most hours.

Approach

The robot is a slider that runs along one end of the table, driven by a NEMA 23 stepper motor through a DM542T driver and an Arduino. When the puck comes at you, the striker slides over to meet it and hits it back. We were happy with it, but we still wanted the original goal of making it see. So we pitched the idea to the Robotics Club, and they gave us a 200 dollar grant to add computer vision, which was amazing.

Build

We took the table home and slowly worked on it in a basement over the following months. We built a tall PVC frame that arches over the table and strapped a phone to the top, pointed straight down. The phone streams to a computer that tracks the puck, and the computer sends instructions to the Arduino telling the robot where to move. My part was mostly the hardware: the camera mount, wiring everything together, and the code that drives the striker. There was also a fun thermal bug. We had overclocked the drive motor for speed and it started overheating, so instead of slowing it down I added a cooling fan.

Result

And it worked. The robot went from something we drove by hand to something that watches the puck and plays on its own. It is probably the project where I most enjoyed watching mechanical, electrical, and software all click together at once, and getting a grant to keep going made me extra motivated.
