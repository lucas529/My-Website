SLA Disconnect Board

A small board that kept the robot's backup batteries from quietly dying.
role
Electrical · PCB design

hardware · PCB

Context

During my electrical engineering internship at Onward Robotics, one of the robots had a backup-battery problem. Its sealed lead-acid backup batteries were draining much faster than they should, and the frustrating part was that the circuit measuring the batteries was itself part of what kept draining them, even when the robot was switched off.

Approach

My fix was a circuit that fully disconnects the backup batteries whenever the robot is powered down, so nothing can quietly pull from them while it sits. I simulated it in LTSpice to check the idea held up, then built it for real.

Build

I started with a hand-soldered protoboard prototype, a bit of a mess of wires, and tested it until it worked. From there I drew up the schematic and laid out a proper PCB in Altium. The board had to fit a specific spot inside the robot, so it came out in an odd, angular shape rather than a tidy rectangle. That first board carried plenty of test points and room around the components to make debugging easy. Once it was proven, I moved to a production version that stripped the test points back out and tightened the spacing, shrinking the footprint until it mounted with two screws instead of three.

Result

The backup batteries went from lasting about a month to lasting about a year, and the design was handed off toward production. It was my first time taking a board all the way from a pile of wires to something a company would actually manufacture, and getting the finished PCB back in my hands was a great feeling.
