Context

This was my IB Computer Science project, and the biggest thing I had built at that point. I wanted a big programmable display, so I made a 30 by 30 grid of individually addressable LEDs, 900 in total, that I could draw on and scroll text across, all from software I wrote myself.

Approach

The hardware is a wall of addressable LED strips wired into a grid and driven by a Raspberry Pi. The fun part was the software. Rather than hard-coding what it shows, I built a whole little app: you can paint a picture pixel by pixel, type out text from a bitmap font I drew letter by letter, queue up several displays with their own timing, and preview all of it on screen before it ever reaches the panel.

Build

The whole thing runs off a breadboard and a lot of jumper wires. The software is where it really grew: a paint tool with RGB sliders, a text creator, an ordering and timing manager, and a live simulator. I even had to map out all the LEDs by hand, since they are wired in a back and forth snake rather than neat rows.

Result

It worked, and I was proud of it. It also grew into one giant file held together with a lot of enthusiasm, which is exactly how it taught me my favorite lesson: plan the structure first. I still look back at it fondly, and it is a big reason I now think a design through before diving in.
