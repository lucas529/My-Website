Electrical Engineering Intern, Hardware Team
My first real taste of engineering in industry — a startup summer where I got to do a bit of everything, from a production PCB to a week of overnight testing out in a warehouse.
SLA Disconnect Board
The robot's backup batteries were draining way too fast, turns out the thing measuring them was quietly draining them too, even when the robot was off. I designed a board to cut them off completely when it powers down, took it from a protoboard to an oddly-shaped production PCB to fit it inside the robot, and pushed backup life from about a month to a year. Once testing was complete I significantly improved the PCB shape to optimize space.

Parts Database Tool
For the intern pitch challenge I was the only one who went technical. The team's parts database was scary-fragile, one accidental select-all-delete could wipe it, so I pitched a safer form-based version. Leadership liked it enough to have me build it, so I taught myself Google Forms and Sheets, made the tool, and wrote a guide for the team to try out.
Clamping Circuit
A 60V protection circuit was burning off more power than some parts could handle. I helped run tests to pin down what was happening, wrote it up in a wiki, and we fixed it by re-picking component values so it stayed within spec.
Second Manual-Mode Button
Small one, but satisfying: the manual-mode button was annoying to reach from the front of the robot, so I was asked to add a second one in the front handle and wrote a protocol to roll it out across the fleet.
Intern Pitch Deck
One of our intern assignments was to pitch ways the company could improve. Most people went cultural or marketing — I went technical, with a deck of process fixes, tweaks to the picker's experience, and some "Lumabot 2.0" hardware ideas, all from watching how the robots actually get used.

Undergraduate Researcher
The only undergrad on a from-scratch CyLab project with Prof. Anthony Rowe, building a safety device for the newly-legal plug-in solar systems.
Contactless Current Sensor
The core idea: read how much current is flowing through a breaker without ever touching it, by taking advantage of how the breaker is built inside.
Project description →
Panel Test Bench
Building a test bench that mimics a real residential panel (code-rated breakers, space-heater loads) to validate the sensor.
Plug-in-play PV Adapter
The other half of the system: an adapter that plugs in between a plug-in solar unit and the wall outlet. The idea is that it talks to the current sensor and cuts the solar unit off from the house circuit if things look unsafe.

Hi, I'm Lucas.

As a kid I wanted to be an inventor, the kind who adds a little to what humanity knows. There's no major for that, so I went with engineering. I chose ECE because, honestly, it felt like black magic. I could reason my way through mechanical things, but circuits just worked, and that bugged me enough that I had to understand why. That curiosity has since spread across the whole field, pulling me deeper into robotics and the ECE projects I find most interesting. Some of that curiosity probably originates from moving around a fair bit. I've lived in eight places, including five and a half years in Brazil, where my family's from.

Outside of engineering, I play tennis, I'm getting into HEMA (historical swordfighting), and I run on tea instead of coffee (coffee and I don't get along), with the occasional round of "For Honor" with friends. Mostly, though, I just like learning random things, like history and philosophy.

what i want to do

Long-term I really want to end up in robotics, but honestly I'd be happy anywhere I get to build real, impactful things and learn from intellectual people. I'm planning to do a one-year master's, and I'm open to internships and full-time roles.
